DESIGN APPROACH

From a question to an experience

Users do not want “motion detected.” They want “I know everything is fine at home.” The project lets cameras already in the home deliver a verifiable conclusion instead of another alert.

A team entry in the Anker Hackathon smart-security track. It reuses the eufy multi-camera system and HomeBase edge compute already in the home, presenting a conclusion card on the first screen. I owned the hardware and inference side: stream capture, on-device inference, the decision service, and cross-camera handoff.

Keep judgment inside the home network

Capture and inference run on HomeBase and the cameras, and conclusions are generated locally. No disease labels, no face recognition — less private data leaves the home.

Give conclusions a confidence level

The conclusion card renders four states: when confident it says “everything is fine at home,” and when not it says “I am not sure,” rather than pushing a vague judgment back to the user.

Stay continuous across cameras

A single target is handed off between cameras, so a pet leaving one frame is not immediately lost and conclusions stay continuous over time.

Features · scenarios · needs · value

Core features

  • On-device conclusion card: the home screen states “everything is fine at home” or “I am not sure” instead of a motion alert
  • Multi-camera capture and HomeBase edge inference: judgment completes inside the home network
  • Cross-camera handoff: a single target is tracked across cameras and is not lost between frames
  • Confidence mechanism: an explicit “I am not sure” when the system is not sure
  • Privacy by design: no disease labels, no face recognition

Application scenarios

  • Pet care while at work or traveling: confirm the cat or dog is fine remotely
  • Homes with several eufy cameras: living room, balcony, feeding area
  • Everyday reassurance about elderly or young family members — explicitly not medical diagnosis
  • Quick outings: a glance that confirms the home is all right

Needs addressed

  • Users want “I know everything is fine at home,” not “motion detected”
  • Alert noise and false positives train users to ignore notifications — they need conclusions
  • Cloud recognition raises privacy concerns; edge inference keeps data at home
  • Cameras work in isolation, with no continuous state across screens

Commercial value

  • Reuses installed hardware: no new camera purchases, activating sold devices and HomeBase compute
  • Shifts value from hardware margin to service: the conclusion card supports a subscription narrative
  • Differentiation through scenario innovation: a verifiable conclusion versus the motion-alert red ocean
  • Privacy as a selling point: on-device processing fits the sensitivity of home security data
